Article: {title}
Publisher: {publisher}
Published: {published} (the article date)

## What counts as a claim

A statement about the future of {company}: a figure it expects (revenue, margins, expenses, capital expenditures, earnings per share, deliveries, users, headcount), a change it expects ("sales will grow next year"), a plan (layoffs, restructuring, buybacks, a capex program, a factory, a product launch date), or a qualitative expectation ("demand will stay strong").

Include every such claim by {company}'s executives, its spokespeople and the company itself, including qualitative ones. Include claims by analysts, unnamed sources and the reporter only when they are specific: a figure, a rise or fall of a measure, or a plan with a date ("Morgan Stanley expects capex of $70 billion next year", "people familiar with the matter said the company plans to cut 5% of staff"). Leave out their general opinions about prospects, competition or valuation. Who made a claim is recorded in `attribution`; claims by others are kept so they are never mistaken for management's.

Do not include:
- Results already reported, or what happened in the past.
- Claims about other companies, the market or the economy, unless they say what will happen to {company}.
- Stock price predictions and ratings ("shares could rise 30%", "a buy rating", "price target of $200").
- Boilerplate (forward-looking statement disclaimers, "subscribe to read more").

## How to fill each claim

- `quote`: the exact sentence or span the claim comes from, copied character for character from the article text below, including any quotation marks inside it. At most two sentences. No ellipses, no added words.
- `attribution`, by who makes the claim:
  - `named_executive`: a named person the article presents as an officer or executive of {company} (CEO, CFO, president, head of a business unit).
  - `company_spokesperson`: an unnamed or named spokesperson, representative or "a person speaking for" {company}.
  - `company_statement`: {company} itself ("{company} said it expects", "the company forecast", "in a statement", "in its filing", "in a blog post").
  - `analyst`: an analyst, investor, rating agency or other outside expert, named or not.
  - `anonymous_source`: "people familiar with the matter", "sources", an unnamed insider, a report by another outlet.
  - `reporter`: the article's own expectation, with nobody cited.
  When an analyst's estimate and an executive's quote are in the same paragraph, they are two claims with different attributions.
- `speaker_name` and `speaker_role`: as the article writes them ("Jensen Huang", "chief executive"; "Ben Reitzes", "analyst at Melius Research"). Use the full name given anywhere in the article when a later sentence uses only the surname.
- `is_direct_quote`: true only when the claim's words are inside quotation marks in the article.
- `venue`: where the statement was made, if the article says: `interview` (with this or another outlet, a podcast, TV), `conference` (an investor or industry conference, keynote, trade show), `earnings_call` (the earnings call or results presentation), `press_release`, `social_media` (X, LinkedIn, a blog post), `other` (a shareholder meeting, memo, filing, letter), `unknown`.
- `statement_date`: the date the statement was made, as YYYY-MM-DD, when the article says it ("on Tuesday", "last week at Computex", "on its earnings call on Oct. 29"); count back from the article date. Null when the article does not say when.
- `claim_type`:
  - `numeric_forecast`: the claim gives a figure or range for a measure in a period ("capex of $64 billion to $72 billion in 2025").
  - `directional`: it says a measure will rise, fall or stay flat, without a figure ("revenue will grow next quarter"). Set `direction`.
  - `plan`: an action the company will take (layoffs, restructuring, a buyback, building a factory, a capex program, a launch, an acquisition).
  - `qualitative`: anything else with no checkable content ("AI is the biggest opportunity of our lives").
- `metric`: `revenue` (total revenue or net sales; segment revenue too, with `segment`), `gross_margin`, `operating_margin`, `operating_expenses`, `operating_income`, `eps` (diluted earnings per share), `capex` (capital expenditures, AI infrastructure spending), `tax_rate`, `other` for any other measure (deliveries, users, headcount, cash flow, data center revenue without the word segment), or null when the claim has no measure.
- `basis`: `gaap` or `non_gaap` when the article says so ("adjusted" is non-GAAP), otherwise `unspecified`. `measure`: `level` for an amount or rate, `yoy_growth` or `sequential_growth` for a growth rate, `share_of_revenue` for a share of revenue.
- `period_text`: the target period exactly as written ("the fourth quarter", "fiscal 2026", "next year", "2025", "this year"); null when none.
- `value_text`: only the figure as written, with its range or approximation words and unit ("$64 billion to $72 billion", "about $37.5 billion", "20%"); null when there is no figure.
- `claim`: the claim in brief, in your own words.
